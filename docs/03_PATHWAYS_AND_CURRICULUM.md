# KidGem pathways and curriculum

## Scope and alignment
This is a proposed product sequence, not an official school curriculum. Grade placement varies by jurisdiction. Before marketing alignment, select a named curriculum, map each skill to its current outcomes and have an educator review the mapping. Each language uses proficiency placement independently from grade, especially for heritage Bengali learners and French beginners.

## Proposed progression
| Grade | Mathematics | Logic | Coding and algorithms | Language development |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counting, comparing, addition/subtraction with objects, shapes | Matching, sorting, repeating patterns | Direction commands, everyday sequences | English sound-letter links; French listening/greetings; Bengali sounds and letters |
| 2 | Place value, regrouping foundations, time, measurement | Missing patterns, simple clues | Debug short sequences, repeat actions | Simple English sentences; French familiar words; Bengali signs and simple words |
| 3 | Equal groups, multiplication, division, fraction models | Classification, deduction, spatial puzzles | Loops, ordered procedures, linear search | Short passages, sentence order; level-appropriate French/Bengali reading |
| 4 | Equivalent fractions, decimals introduction, area/perimeter | Multistep clues, reasoning with tables | Conditions, inputs, algorithm tracing | Paragraphs, grammar in context, supported conversations |
| 5 | Fraction operations, decimals, volume, data | Constraint puzzles, systematic cases | Variables, events, functions; sorting models | Summaries, vocabulary strategies, longer graded stories |
| 6 | Ratios, percentages, integers, expressions | Arguments, counterexamples, networks | Block-to-text transition, lists, search comparisons | Inference, structured writing, purposeful dialogue |
| 7 | Proportions, equations, geometry, probability | Conditional reasoning, logic statements | Functions, decomposition, selection sort and testing | Evidence-based reading, revision, narration and explanation |
| 8 | Linear relationships, exponents, transformations, statistics | Model evaluation, strategy comparisons | Integrated projects, search/sort tradeoffs, introductory efficiency | Analytical reading, extended writing, presentations and projects |

These cells describe future coverage, not a mandate to generate an entire textbook during the first implementation. The content inventory must track planned, drafted, reviewed, published and retired counts by subject, grade and locale.

## Content hierarchy
Subject → Level or grade → Strand → Chapter → Skill → Lesson → Stage → Item. Each skill has a stable identifier, measurable objective, prerequisite skill IDs, teaching examples, common misconceptions and review recommendations. A lesson can teach several related representations but should assess one main objective. Subject paths are directed acyclic graphs; validation rejects cycles and references to missing skills.

## Example pathway for multiplication
Count equal objects → Make equal groups → Interpret repeated addition → Read arrays → Connect arrays to multiplication → Solve equal-group word problems → Introduce division as grouping. If array questions reveal repeated-addition confusion, recommend a short bridge lesson instead of repeating the same question with larger numbers.

## Learning cycle
1. Discover: one visual explanation and a worked example; approximately 1–3 minutes.
2. Guided Try: two scaffolded tasks; assistance is expected and recorded.
3. Play: a short purposeful game with a clear link to the objective.
4. Practice: six scored items with feedback, no default timer and optional hints.
5. Challenge: two new-context tasks that test transfer.
6. Reflect: a short explanation or choice about the strategy, followed by summary and next step.

These are design estimates, not enforced session lengths. Offer a natural stopping point and resume at every stage.

## Mastery rule for the demo
Use transparent states: not started, learning, ready for review and mastered. Completion means all required stages were attempted; it is distinct from mastery. Mark a skill mastered when at least five of six practice items and both challenge items are correct on their first submitted, unassisted attempt in a qualifying assessment session. Hints or retries remain useful learning but do not count as independent evidence. Never lower the original attempt count after reset. Offer another assessment with different item IDs or variants after feedback; do not present an immediately repeated memorized set as new mastery evidence.

Keep all attempts. Preserve the best qualifying evidence and latest evidence separately. Mastery is an instructional heuristic, not a diagnosis or a guaranteed measure of retention. A later review can flag “review recommended” without removing an earned cosmetic reward.

## Next lesson selection
If an unfinished lesson exists, recommend resume. Otherwise find the earliest published lesson at the selected subject level whose prerequisite skills are mastered. If an assessment shows a prerequisite gap, recommend the relevant bridge lesson. If no suitable published lesson exists, explain that the current path is complete and offer review or another subject. Do not point to planned content. Manual parent placement can override the recommendation; record the choice and retain prior evidence.

## Review schedule
Propose review after 2, 7 and 21 days from qualifying mastery. Implement as due suggestions rather than compulsory streaks or notifications. Demo scheduling uses the device date and clearly has no cloud synchronization. Production uses a documented family timezone and tests date boundaries. A short review uses different examples and gives hints after an unsuccessful independent attempt.

## Parent interpretation
Show concrete evidence: “Arrays: 5/6 independent practice answers, 2/2 challenge answers; reviewed on [date].” Show “Needs another try” rather than “weak child.” Never compare siblings, publish rankings or claim IQ growth from the reward balance. Provide one suggested offline follow-up activity per chapter.
